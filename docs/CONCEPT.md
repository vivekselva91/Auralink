# AuraLink concept

## The idea

The Apple home knows what devices it has, but not where you are or what you need. AuraLink gives it spatial awareness, through two products on one foundation:

- **AuraLink for Apple Watch (control):** the devices in front of you rise up on your wrist, and the Crown controls them, with haptics you can feel.
- **Room Sense (care):** camera-free radar sensing that alerts family if someone falls, and tracks presence and sleep breathing.

## Product 1: AuraLink for Apple Watch

**Problem:** Control Center, Siri, and the Home app are fast if you know a device's name. In a room with several lamps, or as a guest, names fail.

**Experience:** raise your wrist, the devices in the direction you're facing appear closest first, double-tap to open the top one, turn the Crown to adjust, with a haptic tick at every step.

**Why the Watch, not only the iPhone:** it's already on your wrist, so control takes a second, and the Crown and double-tap are natural controls. The iPhone does what the Watch can't: mapping the room with its camera.

**Premium hardware (concept):**

| Feature | What it adds | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Software, compass, room map | Facing list, Crown control, haptic ticks | All recent Watches |
| Braked Digital Crown | Real resistance near the limits, hard stops | Premium models, such as Ultra |
| Directional haptic band | 4 motors tell you which way a device is | Optional $129 band |

People reliably feel only about 4 distinct vibration spots around the wrist, so the band uses 4 zones: ahead, left, right, behind.

## Product 2: Room Sense

**Problem:** families worry about parents living alone. A camera in a bedroom or bathroom feels invasive, and a watch isn't worn in the shower or overnight while charging.

**Product:** a small plug-in sensor using 60 GHz radar to detect falls, presence, and breathing during sleep. Alerts go to family through the Home and Health apps.

**Why radar:** it measures distance and motion, so no images exist, and it works in the dark and through steam. It sees the few millimetres of chest movement from breathing, which motion sensors miss.

**Positioning:** safety and wellness alerts, not a medical device. Medical claims, such as diagnosing a sleep condition, would need regulatory clearance.

## How the two products work together

| Shared piece | AuraLink for Watch | Room Sense |
| --- | --- | --- |
| iPhone room map | Device positions for the facing list | Sensor placement and room layout |
| Apple Home | Sends commands to devices | Sends alerts, triggers automations ("lights on when someone gets up at night") |
| HomePod and home hub | UWB anchors for your position | Alert announcements in the home |
| Privacy | All on device | No images; only events leave the sensor |

Room Sense presence can also make AuraLink smarter: if the bedroom sensor knows you just got up at 3 a.m., the Watch can put the dim hallway light at the top of the list.

## How the concept evolved

| Version | Change | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| v0.1 | iPhone pointing with five air gestures | First draft |
| v0.2 | Pointing plus an on-screen haptic dial | Touch beats air gestures once the phone is in hand |
| v0.3 | Apple Watch control, plus Room Sense | The Watch is faster than taking out a phone; Room Sense adds a care product where the need is strongest |
